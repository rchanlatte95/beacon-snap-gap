### Findings from inspecting the files

- **F-1: CDC PLACES social-needs measures are empty for Florida.** In DS-H7, `FOODSTAMP_CrudePrev`, `FOODINSECU_CrudePrev` and `LACKTRPT_CrudePrev` have values for 40 states and no values at all for FL (also CO, KY, OR, PA, SD, TN, TX, VT, WA, WY). NY has 5,289 populated tracts and UT has 710. This explains why transportation data is missing for Central Florida, and it is a direct input to the blind-spot map.
- **F-2:** DS-H6 includes `TractSNAP` and `PovertyRate` in its tract characteristics file, which duplicates part of DS-H1 and DS-H2.
- **F-3: SBA 7(a) and 504 lending per resident does not follow food need by tract.** Over 488 Central FL tracts, Spearman rho between loans per 1,000 residents (all years) and SNAP share is -0.051 (p = 0.26), and with poverty rate -0.021 (p = 0.65). Desert tracts have a median of 1.7 loans per 1,000 residents and nogap tracts 1.8 (Mann-Whitney p = 0.82). 50 of 97 desert tracts are below the median lending, against 49.6% of nogap tracts (Fisher p = 0.82). The only signal is a weak positive link with no-vehicle share (rho 0.136, p = 0.0026), which is a noisy proxy. The result holds under a 2,000 population floor, an ACS 2024 denominator and raw counts. The claim that food need lines up with need for SBA lending is not supported. The data has no measure of need for capital. Sources: DS-X5, DS-H1, DS-H2, DS-H4, DS-H6, DS-X7b, DS-X24. Basis: computed in repo by `scripts/analysis/need_vs_sba_lending.py`. Note: `docs/analysis/need-vs-sba-lending.md`.
- **F-4: The Appendix A small-business gap types add no overlap with Classic Food Desert beyond the shared poverty input.** Over 488 Central FL tracts, with inclusive quartiles for every cut: the Entrepreneurship Infrastructure Gap is in 26 of 97 desert tracts (26.8%) against 26 of 391 nogap tracts (6.6%), Fisher p = 2.0e-07. Both rules use top-quartile poverty, so this overlap is built in. Inside the top poverty quartile (122 tracts) it is 26.8% against 8 of 25 (32.0%), p = 0.62. Capital Access Gap: 14.4% against 11.0% (p = 0.38). Underinvestment Gap: 3.1% against 6.9% (p = 0.24), not stable under another employment definition. Credit Access Gap: 20 of 94 (21.3%) against 26 of 389 (6.7%), p = 9.9e-05, driven by the mortgage denial rate (F-6); inside the top poverty quartile 21.3% against 2 of 24 (8.3%), p = 0.24; with county quartile cuts 13.8% against 8.5% (p = 0.12). Thresholds (labor-force median for "sizable", 20-application floor, employment-population ratio) are choices that need approval (H-5). Sources: DS-B2, DS-X5, DS-H1, DS-H2, DS-H3, DS-X28, DS-H5, DS-H6, DS-H9, DS-X24. Basis: computed in repo by `scripts/analysis/shared_basis.py`. Note: `docs/analysis/shared-basis.md`; tract table `docs/analysis/tract_gap_types.csv`.
- **F-5: Desert tracts have more SNAP retailers per resident than nogap tracts, not fewer.** DS-H8 has no date column, so the date of the list is not known. 1,693 Florida retailers fall inside the 489 tract polygons (point in polygon). Types: Convenience Store 760, Other 435 (410 named Walgreens, CVS, Dollar Tree, Dollar General or Family Dollar), Supermarket 226, Super Store 136, Grocery Store 106, Specialty Store 22, Farmers and Markets 8. Median retailers per 1,000 residents (2020): desert 0.74, nogap 0.46 (Mann-Whitney p = 9.7e-05); supermarkets and super stores 0.16 against 0.00 (p = 0.0011). Zero-retailer tracts: 8.2% against 17.4% (Fisher p = 0.028). Retailers per 1,000 go up with SNAP share (Spearman rho 0.343). The result holds in urban tracts only. The file has no sales, size or ownership data. Sources: DS-H8, DS-H1, DS-H2, DS-H6, DS-X24. Basis: computed in repo by `scripts/analysis/shared_basis.py`. Note: `docs/analysis/shared-basis.md`.
- **F-6: The home mortgage denial rate goes up with poverty and SNAP share, and is higher in desert tracts.** Over 475 tracts with 20 or more applications (DS-H9, HMDA 2025), Spearman rho with poverty is 0.345 (p = 9.3e-15) and with SNAP share 0.434 (p = 3.0e-23). Median denial rate: desert 0.3589, nogap 0.2807 (Mann-Whitney p = 6.2e-11). Inside the top poverty quartile: 0.3589 against 0.2933 (21 nogap tracts, p = 0.026; does not pass the Bonferroni threshold of 0.0083). No clear link with SBA loans per 1,000 (rho -0.082, p = 0.074). This is home mortgage credit, not business credit, and the file has no denial reasons. Sources: DS-H9, DS-H1, DS-H2, DS-H6, DS-X5, DS-X24. Basis: computed in repo by `scripts/analysis/shared_basis.py`. Note: `docs/analysis/shared-basis.md`.

## 5. Known caveats

1. The Florida Policy Institute article is advocacy research. Its figures cite state and federal data, but we should recompute county declines from DS-X1 or DS-X2 before presenting them as facts.
2. Eligibility rules in FACT-11 come from snippets and an explainer site, not the rule text. Confirm with the USDA FNS page before encoding thresholds.
3. No dataset in sections 1 or 3 gives household-level eligibility. "Above-the-line" must stay a tract-level likelihood.
4. Sites that blocked plain fetches were retrieved with a headless browser (DS-X12 and section 6). Values from the FEMA service and the Census Reporter API should still be checked against the publishers' bulk files before we quote them in a submission.
5. We did not download GUIDE-track data (the higher-education starter package). It is outside the Beacon track.
6. The Census Data API (api.census.gov) returned a "Missing Key" page for a request with no key (checked 2026-11-07). Get a free API key before you use DS-X7 or other API pulls. Census QuickFacts returned HTTP 403 (Cloudflare) to automated fetches. Files on www2.census.gov downloaded without problems.

## 8. App source links and exploratory proximity

Updated 2026-11-08. The App provides source links beside findings, proximity controls, tract details, and action briefs. Its expandable source list names each publisher, source period, retrieval method, and material limits. It uses the IDs above; `src/dataSources.ts` is the displayed source catalog.

- REQ-19 / Z-23 uses SBA loans per 1,000 residents (DS-X5 / DS-X24), at or below the inclusive first quartile over populated tracts, and at least 1,200 residents. This is a recorded lending-rate rule, not proof of unmet demand for business credit. The approved rule yields 26 dual-gap and 12 triple-gap tracts; the 97-food-desert definition is unchanged.
- Proximity sliders use a tract's population-weighted center from DS-X24 and straight-line distance to DS-X12 sites. Nearby-site counts and people per nearby site use the same selected radius. These center-based exploratory values differ from the Build's fixed 10-mile aggregation across populated blocks; neither is a measured road-travel-time surface.
- At the user's instruction, the time slider uses the existing 10-mile / 30-minute relationship. This is an assumption-based conversion, without traffic or road routes. It does not change the USDA grocery-access flag or the approved 30-minute road-coverage requirement.
- Source periods are preserved. ACS values cover 2020 to 2024, population is from 2020, SBA approvals span fiscal years 2010 to 2026, the SNAP context uses the saved CBPP series, and county food-insecurity context uses 2024 Feeding America estimates. Missing outcomes remain distinct from zero.
- Publisher pages opened 2026-11-08: SBA DS-X5; Census S2201, B08201, and B19013 definitions; USDA Food Access Research Atlas; Second Harvest Food Finder; CBPP SNAP Tracker; Feeding America Map the Meal Gap; CDC PLACES. FEMA's overview returned HTTP 403; its stored extract and prior retrieval notes remain the basis for that citation. This check did not refresh source data.
