Hackathon Challenges
DataKind is supporting the DevFest Florida hackathon with two challenges featuring Beacon and GUIDE.
Beacon: From Insight to Coordinated Community Action
Participants will use Beacon's free public community data to explore small business support gaps and food access and nutrition gaps across Central Florida, New York City, and Salt Lake City. Teams can develop a prototype, analysis, visualization, or other artifact that demonstrates how public data can help organizations better understand community needs and take action.

GUIDE: Explorations in Higher Education Data
Participants will work with GUIDE's open-source data model and 1.36 million sample records from two Central Florida institutions to explore new applications for higher education data. Potential directions include tools for academic advisors, insights into student pathways and patterns, and what-if scenarios that help students explore their academic plans.

DataKind hackathon judge
DataKind will be a hackathon judge, helping evaluate the ideas and solutions developed by participating teams. 
For the DataKind track, please consider the following themes while building your solution:
Desirability- 40%
 (does a human actually need this?)
Would the small business owner or food bank actually want this? Did participants ground their build in a specific need vs. “a cool thing Beacon’s data could do”?
Viability- 30% 
 (does this serve DataKind’s and Beacon’s goals?)
Does this point toward something worth building further? Does it respect trust, transparency, and the “public good"
Feasibility - 30%
 (is it technically sound?)
Does it work? Is the underlying logic defensible? (Note: This intentionally is not the largest weight category)
DevFest Florida Hackathon Theme
Across all tracks, we challenge designers, devs and builders to consider building solutions that foster learning and educational growth.  As our industry continues to explore implementations of artificial intelligence(AI), we believe it’s vital for us to create systems and culture that amplify the best of humanity.    
We believe that people and interactions matter most over AI tool implementation.
As developers, we believe we have the potential to encourage positive culture and change through the influence of software craftsmanship and creating learning cultures.
Many fun cooking contests often encourage their chefs to include a secret ingredient to help inspire creativity.   For our DevFest, consider using learning as our collective secret ingredient to help inspire positive change across all our solutions.   
What do you want to teach?  What lessons do you want to encourage?  Excited to see your solutions through the hackathon. 
Devs Track -- Fix the Handoff
Build a web app, mobile app, API, or machine learning solution that helps a request reach the right person and move toward resolution. Choose a setting such as equipment repairs at a makerspace, supply requests at a community pantry, or support requests at a campus club.
Core build
Capture a request with enough information for someone to act on it.
Assign an owner or destination through manual selection, rules, or automated suggestions.
Track progress and show the requester the current status and next step.
Keep incomplete or unroutable requests visible for follow-up.
Demo and submission
Submit a request, route it, and move it through resolution. Then show what happens when no suitable owner is available. An API project can demonstrate the same flow through documented requests and responses. Submit the working flow and explain your routing and storage choices.
For a first build, use a form, a request list, and assignment and status controls backed by local storage. For a stretch, detect duplicates, add offline intake, or show how the API handles repeated submissions safely.
