# 3-Minute Hackathon Pitch Skeleton

Adapted for DevFest Florida teams. Original structure from the databricks-challenge toolkit playbook on GitHub (a hackathon demo guide), trimmed to what the teams actually need.

# What it is

A strict timing frame for a 3-minute pitch. Each block has a job. Fill in the blanks, rehearse the drills, and the pitch runs itself.

# The skeleton

| Time | Block | Job | Your content |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 0:00 to 0:20 | Hook plus problem | Name the pain in one sentence. Make the judges feel it. |  |
| 0:20 to 0:30 | One-line solution | State what you built in one breath. No feature lists. |  |
| 0:30 to 2:00 | Live demo | Show the thing working. Narrate what the user is seeing. |  |
| 2:00 to 2:30 | How it works | One diagram or one sentence on the key technical move. Keep jargon low. |  |
| 2:30 to 3:00 | Impact and close | Who this helps and why it matters. End on your closing line. |  |

Closing lines that work: state the outcome, then stop. "So the demo is live, the code is open, and judges, this is what we built in 48 hours."

# How your team uses it

1\. Fill in each block in your own words. Keep the demo block as bullet beats, not a script.  
2\. Run the drills below as a team before Nov 6\.  
3\. One person owns the timer in every rehearsal. The timer is law.

# The drills

Three timed runs. Full pitch, out loud, three times. Note where you speed up and where you stall.  
Cold-judge run. Present to someone who knows nothing about the project. If they ask "wait, what does it do," your hook or solution line needs work.  
Q\&A with 15-second answers. Every answer gets one idea and one example, in 15 seconds. If you need more time, say "happy to show you after" and move on.  
Failure drill. Kill the demo on purpose, mid run. Cut to a backup video or screenshots in under five seconds. Rehearse the exact sentence you say when you switch: "And here is the same flow on video."

# Real-world example: the demo that closes the loop

In 2007 Drew Houston launched Dropbox with a simple three-minute screen recording. He opened with the pain he knew from experience: forgetting his USB drive and being locked out of his own files. Then he showed the product fixing that exact pain live on screen, dragging files into a folder and watching them sync.  
The demo ended where the problem started. The loop was closed. The video earned more than 10,000 Diggs in 24 hours and the beta waiting list jumped from 5,000 to 75,000 overnight.  
Use the same shape for your demo block. Open on the pain, end on the pain resolved, and stop.  
(Source: TechCrunch, "How Dropbox Started As A Minimal Viable Product", 2011\)
