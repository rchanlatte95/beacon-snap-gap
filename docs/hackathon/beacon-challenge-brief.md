Beacon: From Insight to Coordinated Community Action
The Opportunity
Small business owners and food banks across Central Florida, New York City (NYC), and Salt Lake City (SLC) face a shared challenge: the public data that could help them exists, but turning it into aligned actionable insights is a challenge. This hackathon invites you to use Beacon’s free public community data to extend the value of Beacon that could directly help a small business support organization or a food bank do their work better by creating prototype apps, workflows, and demonstrations that can make information more consumable.
“Beacon reflects years of practitioner feedback about what organizations actually need, not simply more access to data, but tools that help teams transparently contextualize information, collaborate across functions, and move from analysis to coordinated action more effectively.” DataKind
A map is a starting point, not the finish line. Over two days, build something tangible: a prototype application, a workflow, an analytics dashboard, or any artifact that demonstrates an idea. This isn’t about writing production software, it’s about showing what’s possible. The prototypes and workflows from this event will also help inform the future of Beacon, but the focus is on building something useful for the organizations and communities this data is about.
What You’ll Get
* Two challenges to choose from (below), pick one or more, you can blend ideas from both, or create your own challenge
* Example public datasets for all three communities. 
* Beacon platform access: Here’s access to a getting started guide and demo including how mapping and team collaboration currently work in Beacon. Use these to see what exists today and where your prototype could extend it. Note: this is for context and inspiration, your prototype itself will be built using the example datasets below, not inside Beacon directly.
* Small business and food access resources for each city (see appendix)
* A DataKind team member on hand to answer questions and share real-world context
* Beacon design brand guidelines
Note: this hackathon works with Beacon’s public indicators only. Beacon also supports integrating an organization’s own private data  programmatic data, but that’s not part of this event, everything will be built on public data.
What Success Looks Like
At the end of the two days, each team shares:
1. What did you make? Show us the prototype, analysis, or artifact.
2. What did you learn? What felt feasible, useful, hard, or worth exploring further?
3. What should happen next? What would it take to take this further?
Share your work over the weekend in this folder and use the presentation template.
What might overall activities look like?
1. Develop an interactive prototype.
   1. Visualize, sort, and surface analytics in a way users can engage with.
   2. Explore how a user could add, edit, or deactivate information within the interface.
   3. Stretch activity: Explore how more than one person could interact with the same view, for example, leaving a note, flag, or question on a specific tract or recommendation that a teammate would see.
2. Create a recommendation engine 
   1. Create a workflow that would propose a recommended action or output
   2. Include user controls for the recommendation. Allow a user to approve, reject, reorder, or replace recommendations; confirm or challenge an inferred need; and provide feedback on recommendations.
   3. Stretch activity: Explore what happens when members on a team disagree on a recommendation, surfacing the disagreement itself rather than silently picking one answer.
3. Prototype a chat interface to engage with the data. 
   1. Explore how a user could ask natural-language questions like, “What resources exist in my neighborhood?”, and “What information are you missing?”
   2. Generate community-facing communication about available resources or local information that could be exported. 
4. Make the system’s reasoning visible.
   1. Explore how a confidence level or a “why this was flagged” explanation could sit alongside any recommendation or insight, so a non-technical teammate can understand it without reading a methodology doc.
   2. Prototype a way to show a data limitation in plain language (e.g., “this number is based on a small sample” or “this data hasn’t been updated recently”) rather than hiding it.
5. Apply relevant Beacon branding to demonstrate how the workflow could fit into the existing Beacon experience.
Challenge 1: Small Business Support Gaps
The challenge: Small business support organizations want to know where entrepreneurs in their community are struggling to get capital, resources, or support, so they can target outreach and programs where they’re needed most. Today, answering that question means digging through scattered public datasets by hand, a slow process.
Who this helps: Organizations like CDFIs, Small Business Development Centers, Chambers of Commerce, and other mission small business support organizations working to close capital and support gaps for entrepreneurs in underserved communities.
What you might build: A solution that takes Beacon’s public small business and economic data (SBA lending, income, employment, and more) for Central Florida, NYC, or Salt Lake City, and surfaces communities that may be underserved, along with the evidence behind that conclusion and a next step an organization could act on. This might look like an interactive map, a recommendation dashboard, or a workflow tool.
Beacon already has a published example of this pattern, a program officer in NYC combining SBA loan activity, household economic indicators, and small business listings to identify underserved businesses and build a targeted outreach strategy. This brief asks you to take that same idea further. How might someone more readily access information? How might that individual insight move into collective sensemaking with a team? How might that shared understanding be turned into action?
Want a more structured starting point? See Appendix A for a detailed framework you can use.
Challenge 2: Food Access & Nutrition Gaps
The challenge: Food banks and food access organizations know their communities through hands-on program knowledge, but connecting that knowledge with public data on poverty, transportation, and grocery access is difficult, especially doing it consistently across communities with different data available.
Who this helps: Food banks, pantries, and nutrition assistance organizations trying to understand where need is highest and whether current coverage is actually reaching those who need it.
What you might build: A solution that combines Beacon’s public food-access indicators (SNAP participation, grocery access, poverty, transportation) for Central Florida, NYC, or Salt Lake City to surface communities where need may be going unmet, with a next step a food bank could act on. This might look like creating heat maps, dashboards, applications, and more. Note: transportation data is currently available for NYC and Salt Lake City only, not Central Florida.
Want a more structured starting point? See Appendix B for a detailed framework you can use.
